Agentic Self-Evolving Solution for Aquatic Microplastics Toxicology Screening

Problem

Screening scientific literature on aquatic microplastics toxicity is time-consuming and difficult to scale because relevant studies must be distinguished from large volumes of partially relevant or irrelevant papers, and classification errors can persist when reasoning strategies remain fixed.

Proposed Solution

Develop a multi-stage agentic literature-screening system that classifies studies by microplastics exposure type and uses an automated feedback loop to analyze its own errors and evolve improved reasoning strategies over successive generations. The system also benchmarks multiple LLMs and an embedding-based approach to balance classification accuracy, inference speed, and cost.

Result: Reached 88.2% classification accuracy and benchmarked 8 LLMs plus 1 embedding approach on accuracy, speed, and inference cost.

FHIR-SetupBench — Verifiable FHIR Environment Construction

Problem

Existing FHIR-focused AI benchmarks primarily evaluate agent capabilities within already configured FHIR environments. They therefore overlook an important prerequisite capability: whether an AI agent can independently construct, configure, and validate a usable FHIR environment from an unprepared starting state.

Proposed Solution

Develop FHIR-SetupBench, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate AI agents on baseline FHIR environment construction. The benchmark presents agents with different initial infrastructure states and evaluates whether they can install, configure, launch, and validate a functioning FHIR R4 environment. Machine-verifiable checks assess process execution, port reachability, FHIR metadata, resource completeness, and clinical smoke tests to determine successful environment readiness.

AgNP Closed-Loop AI Agent — Chemistry Experiment Automation

Problem

Nanoparticle synthesis experiments require repeated interpretation of experimental measurements and adjustment of reaction conditions, while laboratory hardware, analytical instruments, cloud data, and AI reasoning typically operate as disconnected components. Fully autonomous control also introduces safety and reliability concerns when AI recommendations can directly affect physical experiments.

Proposed Solution

Develop a human-in-the-loop closed-loop AI agent that connects reagent pump control, UV-Vis spectroscopy, experimental-data processing, cloud synchronization, and LLM-based recommendations into one coordinated workflow. The system extracts experimental features such as peak wavelength, absorbance, FWHM, and peak count, applies hardware-aware constraints, and requires human approval before critical physical actions are executed.

SandboxGen — Multi-Agent Sandbox Synthesis and Malware Analysis

Problem

Dynamic malware analysis depends on correctly configuring an execution environment for each sample, including operating system, software, architecture, execution parameters, and sandbox settings. These decisions traditionally require expert manual effort, and AI agents can fail at different stages even when their initial static reasoning appears correct.

Proposed Solution

Develop a multi-agent framework in which specialized Scout, Architect, Executor, and Analyst agents collaborate across the malware-analysis lifecycle. The agents acquire and inspect samples, infer execution requirements, configure CAPEv2 sandbox environments, execute and monitor malware, analyze behavioral evidence, localize failures, and adapt using previous execution outcomes to support recovery and more reliable long-horizon operation.

Evaluation: Stage-by-stage evaluation across 131 real-world malware samples localizes failures in evidence acquisition, planning, tool-grounded execution, behavioral interpretation, and recovery.
